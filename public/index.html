How it works, simply

01

Your address is a lock everyone can see

Your private key is the combination. Today's computers would need billions of years to work it out from the lock alone. That's why crypto works.

02

Quantum computers are good at exactly that puzzle

None are strong enough yet, and nobody knows when one will be: maybe years, maybe never. The day it happens is called Q-Day.

03

A different kind of lock fixes it

Hash-based locks rely on maths quantum computers are bad at. Each combination works once, so funds move to a fresh lock every time they're spent. QAULT is building a vault that does that for you.

What the results mean

Shielded
An off-curve address (a program derived address). No private key exists, so there's nothing to recover.
Exposed · empty
The key is public, but there's nothing behind it.
Exposed · low
Under $1,000 behind a public key.
Exposed · medium
$1,000 to $100,000 behind a public key.
Exposed · high
Over $100,000 behind a public key.

"Exposed" describes readiness, not danger today. No quantum computer can do this yet. Solana has a plan to move to post-quantum signatures (Falcon) when it's needed, but hasn't switched it on.
